Cooperative Blimp Swarms for Space-Debris Capture Emulation

Studying selected capture interactions in a terrestrial testbed

＋Project experiment or illustrationAuthor-approved media to be added

Cooperative capture involves more than approaching an object. Robots must coordinate their relative motion, establish a useful interaction, and manage the consequences of contact. This research direction explores robotic blimps as terrestrial platforms for examining selected aspects of those tasks, including interaction with moving or tumbling objects and cable-mediated transportation.

Lightweight aerial platforms offer a setting in which sensing, coordination, and attachment strategies can be investigated together. A study could compare planned relative motion with observed contact behavior, examine how a team distributes roles, and test how capture geometry changes during a maneuver. These are proposed experimental questions; the website should report the testbed configuration and completed milestones only after confirmation.

The emulation scope must remain explicit. Buoyancy, aerodynamic drag, gravity, and actuation constraints shape blimp motion differently from orbital systems. The value of the platform lies in examining selected cooperative behaviors and interactions, with those differences stated alongside each demonstration. Scientific challenge

Coordinate contact with a moving object while defining which aspects of capture the terrestrial platform can meaningfully emulate.

Research approach

Proposed approach: develop relative-motion models, cooperative control, and cable-based interactions; evaluate selected behaviors in a documented terrestrial testbed.

Contributions and evidence

Expected contributions: cooperative capture strategies and an experimental framework with explicit emulation limits. No completed capture result is claimed.
