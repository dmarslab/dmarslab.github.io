Autonomous Aerial Grasping Through Cable Interlacing

Using cables to form a grasp

＋Project experiment or illustrationAuthor-approved media to be added

A cable can connect aerial robots to an object while also acting as the mechanism that establishes a grasp. This project investigates how a team of UAVs can route and interlace cables around an object, form a hitch, and transition toward lifting and transportation. The task links geometry with mechanics: a feasible cable path does not by itself establish that an attachment will sustain the required load.

The research concept brings together coordinated robot motion, cable–cable and cable–object contact, friction, and grasp formation. A useful approach must account for how cable routing changes during the maneuver and how the resulting interaction constrains the robots. Modeling and planning therefore need to inform each other, with physical tests used to assess the assumptions behind securement.

The intended contribution is an integrated understanding of autonomous cable-based attachment and cooperative aerial handling. The associated paper, “From Hitch to Lift,” appears in IEEE Transactions on Robotics; its bibliographic record and cable-interlacing footage were supplied by the author. Specific performance claims are reserved for evidence from the full paper. Historical experiments will retain their original institutional attribution.

Scientific challenge

Form an attachment that is both geometrically achievable and mechanically adequate while coordinating multiple flying robots.

Research approach

Connect cable-routing plans, contact models, and coordinated control; compare the predicted attachment behavior with physical trials.

Contributions and evidence

Intended contributions: interaction-aware grasp formation and an integrated attachment-to-transport workflow. Demonstrated outcomes require source verification.

Related research themes

Cooperative Robotics, Cable Manipulation and Robotic Knotting, Physical Interaction, Dynamics and Control
