Exploratory research direction

Autonomous Robotic Search and Manipulation

Using physical actions to reveal and retrieve objects

＋Project experiment or illustrationAuthor-approved media to be added

Object search becomes a manipulation problem when the target cannot be seen or reached directly. This exploratory direction considers robots that use observations to select actions and use actions to acquire new information. Pushing an obstacle may reveal a hidden object; changing a viewpoint may expose a grasp opportunity without altering the scene.

The research concept connects active visual perception, mapping, mechanical search, grasp planning, and closed-loop decision-making. A central challenge is choosing between gathering information and committing to physical action. Plans must account for what the robot knows, what an action may reveal, and whether the resulting contact or grasp is physically feasible.

This direction extends the laboratory’s focus on physical interaction beyond a particular aerial platform. It offers a way to connect perception and planning with the consequences of manipulation. No completed search system, benchmark result, or platform is asserted. Initial website media should use labeled conceptual diagrams until real experiments are available; subsequent project updates can document the chosen task, operating conditions, observed outcomes, and remaining limitations.

Scientific challenge

Decide when to observe, rearrange the scene, or attempt retrieval under incomplete information.

Research approach

Proposed approach: combine active perception, scene-state estimation, action selection, and grasp planning in a closed loop.

Contributions and evidence

Expected contributions: information-aware search and manipulation strategies. This is exploratory work with no completed accomplishment claimed.
