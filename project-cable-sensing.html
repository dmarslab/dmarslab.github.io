Cable Interaction Sensing and Control

Observing the physical interaction state

＋Project experiment or illustrationAuthor-approved media to be added

A robot’s position does not necessarily reveal the condition of the cable it is manipulating. Similar vehicle configurations may correspond to different tensions, contact locations, or frictional states. This research direction asks which measurements and reduced-order variables can make those differences observable and useful for feedback.

Topics include tension sensing, cable-angle measurement, contact geometry estimation, and transitions between interaction modes. A proposed workflow links sensor measurements to a compact description of the robot–cable–object system, then uses that description to inform control and planning. The scientific challenge is to preserve the variables that affect task behavior while avoiding a model that is too complex to estimate reliably.

Experimental storytelling should connect an observable event to its measurement: a cable becomes taut, a contact begins to slide, or an attachment changes geometry. A synchronized video and sensor trace can explain why robot motion alone is insufficient. Specific sensors, estimation accuracy, control methods, and hardware implementations are not yet confirmed. The website therefore presents this work as a research direction and reserves result statements for documented experiments.

Scientific challenge

Identify interaction variables that are observable from practical measurements and sufficient to support useful feedback.

Research approach

Proposed approach: combine sensing, reduced-order models, and contact-state estimation, then examine closed-loop behavior in experiments.

Contributions and evidence

Expected contributions: interpretable interaction estimates and sensor-informed control. No sensing accuracy or validated hardware capability is claimed.
